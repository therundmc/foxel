# Changelog

## 1.5.0

- Back to simple: just the fox, living its little life in its view. The toy chest, the shelf, the box of keepsakes, the stages and everything they brought (the soft toys, the finds, the hearts in the earth, the neckerchief) are gone.
- Its buttons are back in the title bar of its view: throw a ball, blow bubbles, give a treat, say hello.
- More to do with the mouse, right on it: a click and it reacts to where it was touched, a double click and it twirls for joy, a triple click and it gets the zoomies. A double click on an empty spot still drops its ball there.
- Its ball lives out of the view again: it trots out to get it, comes back in with it, and sometimes takes it away when it has played enough.
- Its affection is still there, and never shown: it is kept as before, and only changes, little by little, how it is with you. Fond of you, it shows hearts and is beside itself when you come back after days away; devoted, it comes to your pointer readily and lies down by it. A bad day makes it quieter, and that is all: it no longer sulks, keeps away from your pointer or walks off when touched, and it is never wary of you.
- Nothing it has lived with you is lost: the life kept by 1.4 carries on.
- New skies to stop for: hot-air balloons rising out of a misty valley at dawn (one of them looks a lot like a fox), whales leaping off a cliff-top meadow in the afternoon, a mother and her calf, a rainbow drawn across the sky by a little star after a sunshower, an old castle on an island in the sky that glides out of the clouds, its waterfalls falling into the mist, and the lanterns of a spirit town at dusk, let go into the night, the first of them from right beside the fox. And it dreams of them afterwards.
- More little things it does on its own: it turns round twice before lying down, tucks itself up like a loaf, makes biscuits, scratches behind its ear, bounces for joy on all fours, gets the hiccups, and goes cross-eyed at a petal that lands on its nose before sneezing it off. Touch its head and it may go shy; once it is fond of you, it rolls over on its back for a belly rub. Its stretch and its yawn are bigger and softer too.

## 1.4.1

- Only the fox and the sky: while it stops to contemplate one, its chest, its shelf and its box of keepsakes draw right out of sight and wait until the sky has gone. There is nothing to do with them in the meantime.
- The box of keepsakes comes out for your pointer as readily as the chest and the shelf do: it was much harder to call.
- The earth it throws up where it digs settles as soon as it has done, instead of lying there for twenty minutes, and it is drawn as a little hole between two low banks.
- What you have built together is safe from updates: the stage you have reached and what it has found for you are kept from one version to the next, and a view that has just opened can no longer write over them.

## 1.4.0

- It remembers you. What you do for it (its meals, playing, a stroke, a treat, the time you keep it company) makes its day a good or a bad one, and builds a bond over the weeks. Your work has nothing to do with it: its affection does not depend on how much you get done. None of it is ever shown as a number: you read it in what it does. With Settings Sync on, its memory follows you from one machine to the next.
- It starts as a wild little thing. The first time, it walks in with its ball in its mouth and puts it down in front of you; it is wary of your pointer and a click makes it jump. One good day together and it trusts you. If you already had Foxel, yours starts from this first meeting too.
- It grows on you, stage by stage. As the bond grows it brings its toys home one at a time, learns tricks with them, shows its hearts (not before it has come to love you, and never on a bad day), brings back little things it has found for you, scratches a heart in the earth, and one day wears a little something round its neck. Each stage is a small party.
- Its toy chest. The buttons in the title bar are gone: nothing stays on screen but the fox. A wicker chest waits at the right edge of its view and fidgets now and then; bring your pointer near and it slides out, holding the toys you can take in your hand. Let go of a toy over it to put it away. The fox uses it too: it fetches its ball from it, tidies a toy left lying about, and when it feels like playing takes back out what you have just put away.
- A little shelf, top right, for what is not a toy: its bag of kibble, the soap bottle, and a cookie, which is its treat now. Click the cookie and one falls from the sky, or take it in your hand and hold it out.
- A box of keepsakes, top left: a photograph for every stage you reach together, and one of each kind of thing it has found for you. Some are rare, and a kind found three times starts to shine.
- Toys to throw about, with real physics: they tumble, bounce off the ground, the walls and the ceiling, knock into each other, and land on its head when it misses. Throw one and it runs after it, leaps for it, does a trick with it and throws it back for you to catch; three catches in a row call for confetti.
- Its mood is on its face, whatever it is doing: its mouth, its ears, its eyes. When its mood changes, a little something shows by its head for a moment: a drop or a small sad cloud, a twinkle or a note. On a bad day it looks away from your pointer, does not come when called and walks off when touched; after a missed meal or an ignored break it sulks with its back to you. It is sad, never cross, and it does not hold it against you: play with it, feed it, stroke it, and it comes round. A meal missed while you were away does not count.
- It was waiting for you. Its welcome is not the same after a night, after a few days or after a couple of weeks, and its toys have moved while you were gone. A few days away cost nothing; after that the bond wears off slowly, and never all the way.
- It catches up: if you commit, push or run something while its view is out of sight, it gives you its reaction when you are back.
- And the smaller things: a toy left out is where you left it when its view comes back, until the next day; it always comes in from the left; a full tummy turns down another bowl; it no longer walks backwards to its ball; and its jumps at soap bubbles are those of a fox.
- Like everything else, none of this reads what is in your files. Its life is kept in the editor's own storage, and goes nowhere but through your Settings Sync.

## 1.3.0

- It follows your work, not just your typing (`foxel.reactToWork`). A commit and it plants a little flag, made more of for the first one of the day. A push and a bird flies the letter away. A merge conflict and it gets tangled in yarn, until you are out of it.
- When things go wrong: a command or task that fails makes it flinch, and three in a row send it under a cardboard box. Something that takes long gets an hourglass turned over, then a pile of pebbles stacked while it waits.
- Errors that remain bring a little rain cloud over its head, a heavier one when there are many, and it clears up and cheers when they are all fixed. This replaces the old panic on new errors (`foxel.reactToErrors`).
- Debugging: out comes the magnifying glass, and it marks your breakpoints. A long stretch of focused typing gets its headband on.
- When an assistant writes your code, it puts its glasses on and supervises.
- And the smaller things: it notices commits coming in, a change of branch, an undo spree, new and deleted files, a big paste (a parcel to catch), your scrolling, a stash (it buries a bone, and digs it up again) and your coming back after hours away.
- Like everything else, none of this reads what is in your files: it only listens to editor events and the names of a few commands.

## 1.2.2

- A place of its own: set `foxel.position` to `editor` and the fox lives in a strip across the bottom of the editor area, under your files, which stays in sight whatever the panel is showing. Its group takes no files, and its buttons are in the editor title bar.
- It is still there when you come back: its view is kept alive while another tab of the panel hides it, so it is where you left it, doing what it was doing. It only makes its entrance again after ten minutes out of sight.
- It stays awake longer: `foxel.sleepAfterSeconds` is now 5 minutes by default instead of 30 seconds.
- A little smaller: `foxel.scale` is now 3 by default instead of 4. A value you chose yourself is kept.

## 1.2.1

- Light themes: the pale things around the fox (soap bubbles, sparkles, Zzz, question marks, the rim of its picture bubbles, drops) are drawn in deeper colours on a light background, where they could hardly be seen. The view follows the theme when you change it.
- Whole at last: its tail when it runs, the tips of its ears and its nose in a few poses reached the edge of the box the fox is drawn in and were cut off there, outline included, which showed badly on a light background. It is now drawn with a margin all round.
- Always in the frame: when its view is too small to show the whole fox at the size you chose, it is drawn smaller instead of being cut off, and what floats around its head stays inside the view when it stands at the very edge.

## 1.2.0

- Day and night: the fox lives by your local clock. It says good morning or good night when it comes in, stretches at dawn, dozes in the afternoon, gets the zoomies in the evening and is drowsy at night. Its fur takes a soft golden light at sunrise and sunset and a blue one at night. Turn it off with `foxel.dayNight`.
- Bedtime: at night it pulls a little basket in from the edge of its view, puts on the nightcap that was waiting in it and falls asleep twice as fast; in the morning it stretches and pushes the basket back out. Typing late at night, it taps along half asleep.
- Meals (`foxel.meals`): it gets hungry at breakfast, lunch, snack time and dinner; a little bird flies its empty bowl in and it begs with a rumbling tummy. Click the bowl or use the new **Fill the Bowl** command and it eats kibble by kibble, then bats the bowl away with a paw. Left hungry too long, it gets sad.
- Water (`foxel.hydrationReminderMinutes`): every hour it has a drink from its water bowl, a reminder for you to drink too.
- Breaks (`foxel.breakReminderMinutes`): after 50 minutes of work without a pause, it brings its ball and waits with a little cup bubble; ignored, it flops down and sighs.
- Party days: a party hat and confetti on Friday afternoons, plus a cake on the anniversary of the install.
- Name: `foxel.name` becomes the title of its view.
- Its needs last: closing the view no longer makes it forget that it is hungry, thirsty or waiting for a break, and reminders that come up while the view is closed wait for you to open it.
- Sleep waits its turn: if it is busy when you stop working, it finishes what it is doing and then falls asleep. Doing something with it, moving your pointer in its view or opening it counts as being there, so it no longer dozes off while you play with it. Once it is asleep, though, it takes a click to wake it.
- Time with the fox is a break: throw the ball and it stops asking for one and starts counting your work again from there, and five minutes spent only with it count as a pause from work.
- The big good morning, good night and party welcome are kept for the first time it sees you; when the view comes back a moment later, it just waves.
- Mouse hunt: a tuft of tall grass comes up, the fox slips in and lies flat with only its head showing, a mouse scurries by, it wiggles and pounces. The mouse is always quicker: either it is gone and the fox lands nose first, puzzled, or it ends up sitting on the fox's head, to be waved goodbye.
- Passers-by: every few minutes in the daytime a butterfly flutters through, and more rarely a little bird crosses the sky or comes down to peck. Half the time the fox cannot resist: it creeps up, wiggles and pounces. The bird always gets away.
- More to watch: it digs, and sometimes it faces you, right up against the glass of its view, rubs it with both front paws and licks it clean.
- Contemplation: once or twice a day, the fox stops for the sky. It breathes in, turns its back to you and watches a whole landscape come out behind it for a long minute. Which one depends on the hour: there are eleven of them, from the sunrise to the northern lights, and each has its great moment, which the fox follows and makes a wish on. Then it turns back to you, lies down and closes its eyes while the view fades; later it may dream of what it saw. If you draw it away to play, the sky goes on to its end without it.
- Soap bubbles: the new **Blow Bubbles** command and view title button send a stream of bubbles up. The fox bursts them with its nose, its teeth, a paw or its tail, jumping, pouncing or spinning in the air, and keeps the last one on its nose. You can burst them too.
- It comes to you: move the pointer into its view and it looks at it, turns, and after a second strolls over and wags its tail. Click an empty spot and it comes running.
- It asks to be petted: rest the pointer on it and, without moving from its spot, it looks up and gently presses its head into your hand.
- No text and no gauges: it shows what it feels with its face, its pose and small picture bubbles.

## 1.1.0

- Touch: the fox reacts to where you click it, with three different reactions each for the nose, head, back, paws and tail (blep, nuzzle, head tilt, play bow, high five, twirl, startled jump, puffed-up tail and more). Clicking again does not restart the reaction; keep clicking and it gets the zoomies.
- Petting now needs the mouse button held down while stroking; just moving the mouse over the fox no longer pets it.
- Eye tracking: the fox follows the cursor with its eyes and turns around if you stay behind it.
- Intro: every time the Foxel view appears, the fox pokes its head in from the edge, looks around, ducks back shyly, then trots in to say hello.
- Ball tricks: after bringing the ball back, the fox sometimes balances it on its nose like a seal, tosses it up and catches it, or rolls it between its paws.
- Dreamy naps: the sleeping fox breathes slowly under rising Zzz, twitches its ears and tail, sticks its tongue out and dreams of the ball, a treat or a butterfly.
- Bouncier, cuter run.
- Plays on its own with a ball lying next to it.
- Treats: new **Give a Treat** command and view title button. A bone falls and the fox lies down to eat it bite by bite. Grab the bone with the mouse to hand-feed it: the fox sits and begs, and takes it once you hold it to its mouth.
- Coat colours: new `foxel.coat` setting with red, arctic, silver and fennec.

## 1.0.0

- First release: a pixel-art fox cub companion that wanders, naps, hunts butterflies, plays fetch, can be petted and reacts to saves, errors, tasks and debugging.
